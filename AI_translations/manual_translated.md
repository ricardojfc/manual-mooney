## Page 5 (German – translated)

**General**

The Mooney M20G "Statesman" is a four‑seat low‑wing monoplane with a
retractable landing gear. It has a 4‑cylinder carbureted engine and is
certified by the FAA in the Normal category.

The airframe is reinforced with steel tubing in the cabin area.

The laminar‑flow wing has a continuous main spar with auxiliary spars. The
front two‑thirds of the wing upper surface use flashing rivets to guarantee a
smooth airflow.

The power plant is a 4‑cylinder Lycoming carbureted engine with 180 hp. A
50 Ampere 12‑volt generator or a 60 Ampere 12‑volt alternator supplies the
electrical loads. The two‑blade constant‑speed variable‑pitch propeller is
hydraulically actuated.

The connection between control yoke and the controls is via push‑pull tubes.
The Mooney Positive‑Control system (PC) is standard equipment. It provides
stabilization about the longitudinal axis and therefore holds a given
attitude for a certain time.

The landing gear allows maximum maneuverability during taxiing. Hydraulic
brakes and a steerable nose gear keep the aircraft in the desired direction
even in strong crosswind. The gear is mechanically retractable. An audible
warning, together with red and green indicator lights, is intended to
prevent landing with the gear retracted.

An electrically retractable landing gear is available for an additional
charge.

**Emergency Procedures – Emergency gear‑extension system** (for electric
gear only)

If the battery is only partially charged, the gear may fail to extend fully
and lock.

1. Pull the electric‑gear fuse (in the fuse panel).
2. Place the electric gear switch in position "gear extended."
3. Push the hand‑crank coupling knob forward to engage the hand crank.
4. Turn the hand crank clockwise until the gear is fully extended and
   locked. Approximately 80 turns.

Afterwards the green "GEAR DOWN AND LOCKED" light must be lit. If the
electrical system has failed completely, the two "G E A R" marks on the
floor window must stand opposite each other.

**CAUTION:**
The gear must not be retracted by hand.

**CAUTION:**
The gear must not be operated electrically while the hand crank is
engaged.

**Positive‑Control‑system**

The pilot can override the Positive‑Control‑system at any time in case of
interference, manually. The Positive‑Control‑system can be switched off by
operating the cut‑off switch on the left control yoke. In the event of
vacuum failure (indicated by the red warning light) the
Positive‑Control‑system is automatically switched off.

**Fires**

*Engine fire on ground start*

Improper starting, e.g. pumping the throttle during a difficult start in
cold weather, can cause a backfire and the subsequent ignition of fuel
collected in the intake duct. In such a case, proceed as follows:

1. Continue cranking the engine with the starter and try to get a start, so
   that the flames and collected fuel are sucked through the carburetor
   into the engine.
2. If the engine starts, run it for a few minutes at 1700 rpm, then shut it
   down and inspect for damage.
3. If it does not start: keep cranking with the starter (booster pump
   switched on) while onlookers prepare a fire_extinguisher.
4. When everything is ready for firefighting, release the starter switch,
   switch off the master and ignition switches, close the fuel‑selector
   valve.

## Page 24 (German – translated)

**Low oil pressure**

If low oil pressure appears together with normal oil temperatures, this
indicates the possibility of a malfunction of the oil‑pressure gauge or of
the relief valve. A leak in the line to the instrument is not necessarily an
immediate reason for a precautionary landing, because an orifice in this
line prevents a sudden loss of oil from the engine sump.

However, a landing at the nearest airfield is advisable to determine the
cause of the malfunction.

If a complete loss of oil pressure occurs together with a rise in oil
temperature, that is sufficient reason to suspect an impending engine
failure. Immediately reduce engine power and look for a suitable field for
a forced landing. During the approach run the engine at low power, being
careful to use only the power really required to reach the chosen landing
spot.

2. Mixture control closed.

**Starting in cold weather**

The starting procedure for a cold engine is the same as for an engine at
normal temperature. Only somewhat more fuel must be injected. It is
recommended to preheat the engine with the aid of a heating unit.

2. Set the brake.

4. Propeller adjustment high rpm; turn the propeller several times by hand

**J.P. INSTRUMENTS – EGT‑701 – 1 GENERAL**

The EGT‑701 temperature indicator displays temperature digitally and in
analog format. The EGT as displayed is based on probes located near the
exhaust outlet for each cylinder and the TIT probe, if installed, is
adjacent to the turbo charger. These probes are not necessarily collocated
with the primary probes; therefore the EGT‑701 may not indicate the same
as the aircraft primary instruments.

The analog display is an electronic bar graph (vertical columns, one per
cylinder) of EGT & TIT temperatures presented as a percentage of 1650 °F.
Below the vertical columns the specific value for EGT and CHT are
displayed digitally. The dot over the column indicates which cylinder's
digital information is presently displayed. The missing bars at the base of
the columns indicate the hottest and coldest cylinder‑head temperature
trend. During Lean‑Find mode the leanest cylinder is displayed along with
the fuel flow (optional) at that time. Depressing the LF and STEP button
simultaneously brings up the adjustable scan‑rate function; OAT in °C or °F.
Depressing the LF button will change the value of the rate or OAT in °C or
°F. Exit by depressing STEP.

If the EGT‑701 buttons are not depressed for 10 minutes the system will
start scanning automatically. Depressing the STEP button will stop the
automatic scan and index through all the functions available. During
constant‑power cruise, if the LF button is depressed for five seconds the
bargraph will level at mid‑scale. The leveled bars represent the peaks of
each column. Each bar represents 10 °F and now acts as an EGT & TIT trend
monitor, quickly showing an increase or decrease in temperature. Depress
again to return to normal; nothing else is affected. With the fuel‑flow
option there is a three‑position toggle switch. The positions are: 1) EGT,
digital and bargraph display of temperatures; 2) FF, digital display of
GPH, REM and USED fuel, temperature bargraph remains; 3) Both, cycles
through everything installed. The data port output sends RS‑232 serial
data every 6 seconds.

Options of Fuel Flow, TIT, OAT, LAT (induction air temp.), OIL, BAT
(voltage) are only displayed digitally with headlines after the number,
as "230 OIL" or "14 GPH". A large value (50+, "CLD") indicates shock
cooling usually associated with rapid descents at low power. Optional
functions not installed will not display. RPM is displayed constantly in
the upper display with no alarms. MAP is shown in the scan display.

**J.P. INSTRUMENTS – EGT‑701 – GENERAL (cont.)**

An alarm causes the digital function to flash as soon as the particular
limit is exceeded. Factory alarm limits for CHT (450 °F) and OIL (230 °F)
are lower than the actual aircraft limits and can not be set by the pilot.
The values may be adjusted to suit individual preference by a qualified
technician. Other factory‑set alarm limits are: "BAT" voltage 15.5/11.0 or
31.0/22.0, Hi/Lo as appropriate; "DIF" (differential, Hi/Lo EGT) 500 °F;
"TIT" 1650 °F Hi; "OIL" Lo 90 °F; "CLD" (rate of change of cylinder head
temperature in degrees per minute) −60 degrees/minute. The pilot should be
aware of the setting of each alarm for his particular aircraft. An alarm
is "canceled" by holding the STEP button in for 5 seconds and seeing the
word "OFF". Then, only that particular alarm is canceled. Canceled alarms
will not appear again until the power has been removed and reapplied to the
EGT‑701. The entire display dims automatically depending on the ambient
light.

The cylinder head with the gasket probe and oil temperature will indicate
generally higher temperatures than instruments provided by the aircraft
manufacturer because the EGT‑701 sensing thermocouples are not collocated
with the primary instrument sensing probes. Therefore, airplane flight
manual limitations based on primary instrument indication take precedence
over those of the EGT‑701.
